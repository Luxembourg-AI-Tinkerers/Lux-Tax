## 1. Conclusion summary (as of the 2026-11-08 audit — historical)

**The app’s 2025 Class 1 barème does not match the official Art. 118 LIR tariff.** The first six non-zero brackets (0 % to 12 %) are correct; every bracket from 14 % upward uses a uniform €2,205 step, whereas the official tariff switches to €2,295 steps (two-point jumps) and different widths for the 39–42 % brackets. The 42 % threshold happens to match (€234,870); the 39/40/41 % thresholds do not. On a Class 1 income of €50,000 the app charges €7,460.10 against an official €7,341 (overcharge €119); on €300,000 the overcharge is about €333.

**Class 1a is not the official formula at all.** Art. 120bis LIR (rewritten by the law of 20 December 2024, in force for tax year 2025) applies the Art. 118 tariff to a *reduced* base `R′ = R − (79,380 − R)/4`, with marginal-rate caps. The app instead computes `(Class 1 + Class 2) / 2`. That is neither the pre-2025 “difference” method nor the 2025 method. It over-taxes low-income Class 1a taxpayers (€705 at €26,460 where the official tax is **0**) and under-taxes higher incomes (about €3,694 too little at €150,000).

**Class 2 splitting is directionally correct** (`2 × Tarif(R/2)`) and reproduces the ACD worked example only if the official brackets are used. With the app’s brackets it fails the ACD example: €14,920.20 instead of **€14,682** on €100,000.

**The 7 % / 9 % fonds pour l’emploi thresholds in the app match the ACD rule** and did not change for 2025 or 2026.

**2026 uses the same Art. 118 tariff as 2025.** No law after the loi du 20 décembre 2024 (Mémorial A 589) has rewritten Art. 118. The ACD barème page still publishes only the “à partir de l’année d’imposition 2025” scale. A conjoncture tax credit runs 1 June–31 December 2026; it is not a scale change.

**Overall confidence: high** on the official brackets, Class 2 splitting, Class 1a statutory formula, fonds thresholds, and the fact of non-conformity. Medium on the exact numerical Class 1a *cote* at every euro (the marginal-cap implementation is the natural reading of Art. 120bis; I could not retrieve the ACD “formules du calcul automatisé” PDF to cross-check every published cote).

### Finding 3 — Class 2 (quotient conjugal / splitting), Art. 121, and rounding — **[historical: resolved]**

**Statute (Art. 121 LIR):**

> « L’impôt à charge des contribuables de la classe 2 correspond au double de la cote qui, par application du tarif prévu à l’article 118, correspond à la moitié du revenu imposable ajusté. »

**Rounding — it is not in Art. 119.** Current Art. 119 (p. 187) only assigns taxpayers to classes 1 / 1a / 2. The rounding rules live elsewhere:

- **Art. 126 al. 2** (p. 198): « Avant l’application du tarif, le revenu imposable est arrondi au multiple inférieur de 50 euros. » → round the *full* taxable income **down** to the next multiple of €50 *before* applying the tariff. This applies to every class.
- **Art. 124** (p. 196): « Les cotes d’impôt sur le revenu déterminées selon les articles 118 à 121 sont arrondies au multiple inférieur d’un euro. » and « Les cotes d’impôt inférieures à 12 euros sont considérées comme nulles. » → round the *tax cote* **down** to the euro; cotes below €12 are zero.
- Art. 121 does **not** re-round `R/2` to a multiple of €50. The tariff is a legal function of any euro amount. The published Mémorial barème table is presented on a €50 grid for readability / withholding; that grid is not an extra statutory rounding of the half.

**ACD official example** (Splitting page, last update 22 January 2025), tax year 2025, excluding fonds pour l’emploi:

- R = €100,000 → half = €50,000 → Class 1 cote = **€7,341** → Class 2 = 2 × €7,341 = **€14,682**.

Independent recomputation with the Art. 118 walls gives Tarif(50,000) = €7,341.30, floored to €7,341 (Art. 124). The ACD figure is therefore reproduced *exactly* by the official brackets plus Art. 124. The app’s brackets give Tarif(50,000) = €7,460.10 and Class 2 = €14,920.20 — **€238.20 too high**, and the ACD example fails.

**App implementation** (`scale2025.ts:72-77`):

```ts
if (taxClass === TaxClass.CLASS_2) {
  const halfIncome = Math.floor((roundedTaxableIncome / 2) / 50) * 50;
  const halfTax = calculateProgressiveScaleTax(halfIncome);
  return Math.round(halfTax * 2 * 100) / 100;
}
```

Deviations from the statute:

1. Extra `floor(half / 50) * 50` on the half — not in Art. 121. Usually immaterial (€0–€49 of base) but not authorised.
2. Tax rounded to cents with `Math.round`, not floored to the euro (Art. 124 al. 1).
3. No nullification of cotes < €12 (Art. 124 al. 2).
4. Underlying brackets are wrong (Finding 2), which is what breaks the ACD €14,682 example.

**Evidence:**  
- Art. 121, p. 190; Art. 124, p. 196; Art. 126 al. 2, p. 198 of the LIR coordonné PDF cited above.  
- ACD Splitting: https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/s/splitting.html  
- App: `packages/tax-engine/src/scale2025.ts:69-77`.

**Grade:** primary statute + ACD worked example. **Confidence: certain** on the legal method and on the ACD example; **high** on the extra half-rounding being a (minor) deviation.

### Finding 4 — Class 1a: who qualifies (Art. 119) vs. how tax is computed (Art. 120bis) — **[historical: resolved]**

**Who is in Class 1a (Art. 119 n° 2), provided they are not Class 2:**

- a) personnes veuves (widows / widowers);
- b) persons who receive a modération d’impôt pour enfant under Art. 122 in the conditions of Art. 123;
- c) persons who have completed their 64th year at the start of the tax year (i.e. aged 65 on 1 January of the tax year).

**Who is in Class 2, not 1a (Art. 119 n° 3):**

- a) persons taxed collectively under Art. 3 or 3bis;
- b) veuves whose marriage ended by death in the three years preceding the tax year;
- c) divorced / legally or factually separated persons if the divorce or separation occurred in the three years preceding the tax year, provided they had benefited from that rule (or a similar earlier rule) for five years before that.

Practical correction to the audit prompt’s shorthand “veufs, divorcés avec enfants, 65+”:  
- A **divorcé sans enfant** is Class **1**, not 1a (and may still be Class **2** for up to three years after the divorce under Art. 119 n° 3 c). Children put a divorced parent into 1a only via the *modération d’impôt pour enfant*, not because of the divorce.  
- A **veuf** is 1a, except during the three-year Class 2 window after the death of the spouse.

**Official Class 1a formula — Art. 120bis LIR** (replaced by Art. 4 of the loi du 20 décembre 2024; applicable from tax year 2025). Quoted in full:

> « L’impôt à charge des contribuables de la classe 1a est déterminé par application du tarif au revenu imposable ajusté réduit d’un quart de son complément à 79 380 euros, sous réserve que le taux d’accroissement maximal ne puisse pas dépasser 39 pour cent pour la tranche de revenu comprise entre 51 804 euros et 117 450 euros, 40 pour cent pour la tranche de revenu comprise entre 117 450 euros et 176 160 euros, 41 pour cent pour la tranche de revenu comprise entre 176 160 euros et 234 870 euros et 42 pour cent pour la tranche de revenu dépassant 234 870 euros. »

**Operational reading (the only reading that reproduces the official €26,460 Class 1a/2 exemption and the 51,804 cap start):**

1. Round R down to the next €50 (Art. 126 al. 2).  
2. Compute the reduced base:  
   - if R ≤ 79,380: `R′ = R − (79,380 − R) / 4`  i.e. `R′ = 1.25·R − 19,845`  
   - if R > 79,380: `R′ = R` (no positive “complément à 79 380”)  
3. Apply the Art. 118 tariff to `R′`.  
4. Cap the *marginal rate of the Class 1a tax with respect to the original R* at 39 % / 40 % / 41 % / 42 % in the R-bands listed in Art. 120bis. Without this cap the `×1.25` slope would push marginal rates above those maxima (e.g. 1.25 × 32 % = 40 % starting exactly at R = 51,804, which is where `R′` = €44,910, the 32 % wall).  
5. Round the cote down to the euro (Art. 124); null if < €12.

**Why 79,380 and 19,845:** 79,380 = 3 × 26,460 = 6 × 13,230; 19,845 = 79,380 / 4. At R = 26,460, `R′ = 13,230` → tax 0. That is the official “revenu imposable ajusté exempté de 26 460 euros en classe 1a” (ACD / Financial Services Luxembourg). At R = 79,380 the reduction vanishes and Class 1a meets Class 1.

**This is a 2025 rewrite.** The pre-2025 Class 1a method (Class 1 tax minus a fraction of the Class 1 − Class 2 difference, fraction 100 % / 50 % / 30 % / 15 % / 0 % across €45k / €90k / €105k / €120k) is **not** the law for 2025 or 2026. The app implements a third, unofficial hybrid: always `(Class 1 + Class 2) / 2`, then `min` with Class 1.

**App code** (`scale2025.ts:79-87`):

```ts
if (taxClass === TaxClass.CLASS_1A) {
  const class1Tax = calculateProgressiveScaleTax(roundedTaxableIncome);
  const halfIncome = Math.floor((roundedTaxableIncome / 2) / 50) * 50;
  const class2Tax = Math.round(calculateProgressiveScaleTax(halfIncome) * 2 * 100) / 100;
  const class1aTax = Math.round(((class1Tax + class2Tax) / 2) * 100) / 100;
  return Math.min(class1Tax, class1aTax);
}
```

The app **over-taxes poor Class 1a households** (single parents, 65+, recent widows past the Class 2 window) and **under-taxes middle/high Class 1a incomes**. Both directions are material.

### Finding 5 — Solidarity surcharge (contribution au fonds pour l’emploi): 7 % / 9 %

**Official rule (ACD, unchanged since tax year 2013; no 2025 or 2026 change).** The impôt fixé selon le tarif is increased as follows, based on *revenu imposable ajusté*:

| Revenu imposable ajusté | Class | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ €150,000 | 1, 1a **and** 2 | **7 %** |
| > €150,000 | 1 and 1a | **9 %** |
| > €300,000 | 2 | **9 %** |

The rate applies to the **whole** tarif tax, not only to the slice above the threshold. Class 2 keeps 7 % up to €300,000 of household taxable income (the doubled threshold is a Class 2 feature).

**Legal basis:** the rates were introduced for 2013 by the loi du 21 décembre 2012 (Mémorial A n° 270). The LIR coordonné refers to “la contribution au fonds pour l’emploi” (e.g. Art. 152, Art. 164bis) without restating the 7/9 percentages; ACD’s A-to-Z page is the current official administrative statement of the thresholds. I could not pull a readable legilux HTML of the 2012 law in this run (Legilux returned a JS shell).

**App implementation** (`engine.ts:235-241`) is **correct** on thresholds, class-split, whole-tax application, and computation on `baseTax` before credits:

```ts
const highIncomeThreshold = taxClass === TaxClass.CLASS_2 ? 300000 : 150000;
const solidaritySurchargeRate = taxableIncome > highIncomeThreshold ? 0.09 : 0.07;
const solidaritySurchargeAmount = Math.round(baseTax * solidaritySurchargeRate * 100) / 100;
```

`taxableIncome > 150000` (strict) matches ACD’s “≤ 150 000 → 7 %” at the boundary. Class 2 at €150,000–€300,000 stays at 7 %, as required.

**2025/2026 changes:** none found. ACD page last updated 13 February 2024 still states “depuis l’année d’imposition 2013”. Financial Services Luxembourg (October 2026) repeats the same 7/9 table. The 2026 conjoncture credit and the 2027 indexation do not touch these rates. A draft reform of the fonds financing is discussed in the press (July 2026) but is not law.

**Evidence:**  
- ACD Fonds pour l’emploi: https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/f/fond_empl.html  
- Loi du 21 décembre 2012: https://legilux.public.lu/eli/etat/leg/loi/2012/12/21/n2 (shell only in this run; existence and 2013 effect confirmed by ACD).  
- App: `packages/tax-engine/src/engine.ts:235-241`.

**Grade:** ACD (official administration) for current rates; app code. **Confidence: certain** that the app matches ACD and that 2025/2026 brought no rate or threshold change.

### Finding 6 — Answer to the audit question: does the app 2025 scale match the official barème? — **[historical: resolved; the current answer is “yes”]**

**No.** Precisely:

1. **First six taxed brackets (8–12 %) and the 0 % ceiling (€13,230): yes.**  
2. **Everything from 14 % to 41 %: no.** The app extrapolates a €2,205 ladder that the 2024 reform replaced with €2,295 two-point steps and different 39–42 % walls.  
3. **42 % start (€234,870): yes**, by coincidence of the last wall.  
4. **Class 1a formula: no** — it is a non-official midpoint, not Art. 120bis.  
5. **Class 2 method: yes in form** (`2 × Tarif(R/2)`), **no in result** (wrong Tarif; extra half-rounding; wrong euro-rounding).  
6. **Fonds pour l’emploi 7/9: yes.**  
7. **Art. 119 rounding: the premise is wrong.** Art. 119 does not contain rounding. Income → floor €50 is Art. 126 al. 2 (app does this). Tax cote → floor €1 and < €12 → 0 is Art. 124 (app does neither).

The engine is labelled “2025” and hard-codes one scale (`scale2025.ts`, engine provenance string “2025 progressive scale”). Because 2026 uses the same Art. 118 numbers, a *corrected* 2025 scale would also be the 2026 scale. The engine does not model the H2 2026 crédit d’impôt conjoncture.

**Grade:** mixed primary code + primary statute. **Confidence: certain.**
