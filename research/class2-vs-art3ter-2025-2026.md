# Luxembourg Class 2 vs Art. 3ter — married / PACS residents, 2025–2026

**Research date:** 8 November 2026  
**Scope:** resident married and PACS couples, tax years 2025 (filed in 2026) and 2026.  
**Household shape in the parent audit:** one spouse is an employee; the other (or the same person) runs a side-business (libérale, commerciale, e-commerce, AirBnB, YouTube, etc.).  
**Rule for figures:** no invented worked examples. The only full numeric examples are the ACD splitting example, the ACD 3ter/2 réallocation example, and arithmetic recomputed from the official Art. 118 tariff (labelled as such).  
**Overall confidence:** **high** on class assignment, election mechanics, Art. 3 / 3bis / 3ter, AEP Art. 129b, 111bis/111ter caps, RCP Art. 110, 90 % tests, 3-year Class 2 transition, CIM exclusion, and the Art. 121 splitting method (statute + ACD). **Medium-high** on the convexity result (Class 2 ≤ any Class 1 split of the same joint RIA) and on the Art. 48 n° 2–3 individual-assessment reading. **Medium** on PACS Art. 48 exception practice (statute is clear; no ACD worked example found).

---

## 1. Conclusion summary

Married residents are **collectively taxed in Class 2 by default**. The only way out is a **joint, initially revocable, then irrevocable Art. 3ter election** (pure individual or individual with réallocation), filed by **31 December of N+1**, which forces *imposition par voie d’assiette* (modèle 100). PACS partners are the opposite: **separate Class 1 / 1a by default**; Class 2 only on a joint request if the partnership and common home lasted the whole year.

On the **barème alone**, Class 2 never loses to Art. 3ter on the same joint revenu imposable ajusté: Art. 121 is `2 × Tarif(R/2)`, and the Art. 118 tariff is convex, so 50/50 splitting is the tax minimum. Art. 3ter al. 3 with the default 50/50 réallocation **reproduces the Class 2 cote** (each spouse is Class 1 on R/2). Class 2 therefore **wins when incomes are unequal** (employee + profitable side-business), **wins on loss offsetting**, and **wins on every household-doubled forfait** (540 / 480 / 672 / 1 344 / 1 500 / AEP 4 500 / fonds 7 % up to 300 k). It **loses only off the barème**: when a Class 1a + CIM alternative exists (PACS, mixed couple without the 90 % option, the 3-year post-divorce/death window), when Art. 48 n° 2–3 block deducting spouse remuneration/rent in a Class 2 business, or when the household cannot or does not want joint liability.

The **2025 Class 1a reform** (Art. 120bis rewritten by the loi du 20 décembre 2024) did **not** create a case where Class 1a beats Class 2 on the tariff. Class 1a is a single-person concession; married people who elect Art. 3ter get **Class 1, not 1a**, even if they have children or are 65+. Recomputation from Art. 118: at every tested RIA from €26 460 to €300 000, Class 2 ≤ Class 1a, and the gap widens with income (Class 2 − Class 1a ≈ **−€93** at €30 000, **−€12 082** at €100 000). The reasons to leave Class 2 are **CIM (€3 504 refundable, Class 1a only)** and **Art. 48**, not the scale.

**Confidence: 高置信 / high.**

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Who is Class 2, who is Class 1 / 1a — the assignment matrix

**Fact.** Art. 119 LIR assigns three classes. Class 2 is collective taxation under Art. 3 or 3bis, plus a 3-year transition after death or qualified divorce/separation. Class 1a (if not Class 2) is widow(er), person with a modération d’impôt pour enfant, or person who has completed their 64th year on 1 January. Everyone else is Class 1.

**Evidence.** Art. 119 LIR 2025, p. 187 (local PDF `.tmp-lir/lir2025.pdf`, page 187):

> « 1. La classe 1 comprend les personnes qui n’appartiennent ni à la classe 1a ni à la classe 2.  
> 2. La classe 1a comprend … a) les personnes veuves, b) les personnes qui bénéficient … d’une modération d’impôt pour enfant … c) les personnes ayant terminé leur 64e année au début de l’année d’imposition.  
> 3. La classe 2 comprend: a) les personnes imposées collectivement en vertu des articles 3 ou 3bis, b) les personnes veuves dont le mariage a été dissous par décès au cours des trois années précédant l’année d’imposition, c) les personnes divorcées, séparées de corps ou séparées de fait … au cours des trois années précédant l’année d’imposition, si avant cette époque et pendant cinq ans elles n’ont pas bénéficié de la présente disposition ou d’une disposition similaire antérieure. »

ACD resident class table (read 8 Nov 2026): [impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/c/class_resid.html](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/c/class_resid.html) — same matrix, including the Art. 3ter rows (individual pure or avec réallocation → **Class 1 each**, even with children or 65+).

**Grade:** primary statute + ACD A–Z. **确定 / certain.**

**App hook.** The engine must not infer Class 2 from “two members in the household”. Married ≠ automatic 2 if a valid 3ter election exists; PACS ≠ 2 unless the joint request is made; divorced/veuf ≠ 1a during the 3-year window.

### 2.2 Married residents: Class 2 is the default, not an option

**Fact.** Art. 3 LIR imposes collective taxation on:

- a) spouses who are residents at the **start** of the year and not *de facto* separated under a legal or judicial dispensation;
- b) residents who **marry during** the year;
- c) spouses who **become residents during** the year (not *de facto* separated);
- d) **on joint request only**, one resident + one non-resident, not *de facto* separated, if the resident spouse realises **≥ 90 %** of the household’s professional income **in Luxembourg**.

Circulaire L.I.R. n° 3/1 du 24 mai 2023: collective taxation is **droit commun**. To escape it, **both** spouses must jointly request Art. 3ter (pure or avec réallocation) by **31 December of N+1**. Silence or disagreement → Class 2. *Séparation de fait* requires both a judicial dispensation **and** a real rupture of *toit, table et lit*; a mere second address, prison, or Art. 215 Code civil separate-residence authorisation without divorce proceedings does **not** break collective taxation.

**Evidence.**

- Art. 3, LIR 2025 p. 13 (local extract, see §2.1 PDF).
- [Circulaire L.I.R. n° 3/1 du 24 mai 2023](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/dam-assets/fr/legislation/circulaires/lir-3-1-du-24052023.pdf), paragraphs “L’imposition collective”, “La séparation de fait”, “La dispense de la loi ou de l’autorité judiciaire”. Example in the circular: jugement de divorce after 1 January → still collective for that year, unless Art. 3ter is requested. Conjoint who files for divorce on 15 December and is authorised to live separately on 3 January cannot invoke that separation for the new year.
- [ACD imposition individuelle](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/i/imposition-individuelle.html): “Les contribuables résidents mariés sont, par défaut, imposables collectivement … en classe d’impôt 2.”
- Form 100 F 2025, page 3 (E/R), local `100f-2025.pdf`: “À défaut de remplissage des cases 316 et l’une des cases 317 à 319, les contribuables mariés seront imposés collectivement à moins qu’ils n’aient exprimé conjointement avant le 31 décembre 2026 un autre choix.”

**Grade:** statute + circular + form. **确定 / certain.**

**Marriage during the year.** Art. 3 b) puts the couple in collective taxation for that year. Art. 3ter remains available until 31 December N+1. There is no prorata of the class: the year is Class 2 unless they elect out.

### 2.3 Art. 3ter — pure vs avec réallocation

**Fact.** Art. 3ter (LIR 2025 p. 15) is a *dérogation* to Art. 3, **joint request**, deadline **31 December N+1**, forces assiette. If made before or during the year, it can still be changed until that same deadline (provided no bulletin d’impôt has been issued). After that, the last conforming request is **irrevocable for that year**. Next year the three-mode menu reopens.

**Individual pure (al. 2).** Each spouse is taxed on **own** RIA, **Class 1** each. Child modération: allocations/CEDIES/SNJ are *deemed granted to both*; *dégrèvement* Art. 122 al. 3 is split **50/50**; child interest/plafond majorations and child Art. 4 income are split **50/50**. Dérogation to Art. 119 n° 2 b–c: **no Class 1a**.

**Individual avec réallocation (al. 3).** First compute **joint RIA the same way as collective** (same forfaits, same plafonds, Art. 48 restrictions apply). Then split. Default **50/50**. Other splits allowed (form 100 cases 322–323, must sum to 100). Each spouse **Class 1**. Other dispositions follow collective.

**Evidence.**

- Art. 3ter, LIR 2025 p. 15 (full text extracted).
- [Circulaire L.I.R. n° 3ter/1 du 24 mai 2023](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/dam-assets/fr/legislation/circulaires/lir-3ter-1-du-24052023.pdf) — pure: attribution of professional income to the earner; capital/rental follow property (community légale 50/50 on common property, biens propres to the owner; séparation de biens to the owner); DS follow the person who meets the legal conditions even if paid by the other (*abgekürzter Zahlungsweg*); 111bis deducted by the souscripteur; social security and RCP by the affiliated spouse; AEP **2 250 € each**.
- [Circulaire L.I.R. n° 3ter/2 du 24 mai 2023](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/dam-assets/fr/legislation/circulaires/lir-3ter-2-du-24052023.pdf) — réallocation: joint RIA first, then split; **50/50 “évite qu’une partie du revenu commun ne soit imposée à un taux marginal plus important”**; Class 1 not 1a; modération *dégrèvement* and bonification follow the chosen percentage.
- [ACD imposition individuelle](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/i/imposition-individuelle.html).
- Form 100 F 2025 p. 3: boxes **317** collective / **318** pure (cases 320–321) / **319** réallocation (cases 322–323); **both signatures** required on page 20. Assistant électronique cannot be used to make this election (see [research-filing-2025-2026.md](../research-filing-2025-2026.md) §assistant).

**Grade:** statute + circulars + form. **确定 / certain.**

#### Worked intuition — réallocation 50/50 = Class 2 on the barème

Class 2 (Art. 121): `2 × Tarif(R/2)`.  
Réallocation 50/50, each Class 1: `Tarif(R/2) + Tarif(R/2) = 2 × Tarif(R/2)`.

They are the **same cote**. The remaining differences are forfaits/AEP/Art. 48/class-1a/CIM, not the scale. A non-50/50 réallocation is **never better** on a convex tariff: ACD’s own example (3ter/2) is a warning, not an invitation:

> Revenu commun 100 000 (A 40 000, B 60 000), chosen split A 75 % / B 25 % → A 75 000, B 25 000.

That split *increases* A’s marginal rate. ACD states the 50/50 default exists precisely to avoid that. Use a non-50/50 split only for a non-convex reason (credit cliff, Art. 48, fiche de retenue, CIM is not in play for married).

**ACD official splitting example (the only full barème example ACD publishes):** RIA €100 000, AY 2025, fonds excluded: Tarif(€50 000) = **€7 341** → Class 2 = **€14 682**. Source: [ACD Splitting](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/s/splitting.html), last update 22 January 2025. Same figure is reproduced by Art. 118 walls + Art. 124 floor-to-euro (see [docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md](../docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md) Finding 3).

### 2.4 When Class 2 wins (and when it does not) — unequal vs similar incomes

**Fact.** For a **fixed joint RIA** run through the Art. 118 tariff, Class 2 is the **minimum**. This is Jensen’s inequality on a convex tariff: `Tarif(a) + Tarif(R−a) ≥ 2 × Tarif(R/2)`. Individual pure is a particular unequal split (the *actual* a and R−a). Réallocation is a chosen split. 50/50 réallocation ties Class 2. Every other split loses.

**Recomputed illustrations from the official Art. 118 tariff** (Art. 126 floor to €50, Art. 124 floor to €1; not ACD worked examples; fonds excluded; 2025 = 2026 tariff):

| Joint RIA | Class 1 on the whole | Class 1a (Art. 120bis reduced base) | Class 2 `2×Tarif(R/2)` | Class 2 − Class 1a |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| €26 460 | €1 409 | **€0** | **€0** | 0 |
| €30 000 | €1 998 | €375 | **€282** | −€93 |
| €40 000 | €4 232 | €2 025 | **€1 224** | −€801 |
| €50 000 | €7 341 | €4 968 | **€2 412** | −€2 556 |
| €79 380 | €18 711 | €18 711 | **€8 282** | −€10 429 |
| €100 000 | €26 764 | €26 764 | **€14 682** (ACD official) | −€12 082 |
| €150 000 | €46 590 | €46 590 | **€34 028** | −€12 562 |
| €300 000 | €108 479 | €108 479 | **€93 180** | −€15 299 |

Script logic: walk Art. 118 walls (8–12 % step €2 205; 14–38 % step €2 295; 39/40/41/42 % wide bands); Class 1a base `R′ = R − (79 380 − R)/4` for R < 79 380, else R. ACD checks pass: Tarif(50 000) = 7 341; Class 2(100 000) = 14 682.

**Employee + profitable side-business (the parent-audit household).** Salary Art. 95 + bénéfice Art. 14/91 are cumulated in Class 2, then split. That is the unequal-income case where Class 2 wins **most**. Illustration from the same tariff (not a full invented dossier):

- One earner €80 000, other €0: Class 1 on €80 000 = **€18 964**; Class 2 on €80 000 = **€8 464**. Class 2 saves ~€10 500 of tarif.
- Two earners €40 000 + €40 000: individual pure = 2 × €4 232 = **€8 464**; Class 2 on €80 000 = **€8 464**. Tie, as convexity requires.

So: **unequal → Class 2 wins big; similar → Class 2 ties individual on the barème.** Class 2 never *loses* on the scale at constant RIA.

**Where Class 2 can still lose money:**

1. **CIM / Class 1a alternatives** (§2.8, §2.9) — off-barème, and only for taxpayers who can be 1a.
2. **Art. 48 n° 2–3** (§2.12) — spouse salary/rent inside a Class 2 business is not a dépense d’exploitation.
3. **Per-person credits that are not doubled in Class 2** — CIS/CIP/CII are per person in every mode (no loss). CIM is simply unavailable to married people in every mode (no Class 2 *loss* versus Art. 3ter; a Class 2 *loss* versus staying PACS-separate).
4. **Fonds pour l’emploi** — Class 2 7 % up to **€300 000** joint vs 9 % above €150 000 for Class 1/1a. Unequal incomes in the €150k–€300k joint band: Class 2 keeps 7 %, the high earner alone would pay 9 % on the whole cote. Class 2 wins. Equal incomes around the cliff: tie. No reverse case found.

**Evidence.** Art. 118/121, LIR 2025 pp. 186, 190; Art. 120bis, p. 189; ACD Splitting URL above; fonds rule ACD barèmes / [research-filing-2025-2026.md](../research-filing-2025-2026.md) §2.6. Convexity is a mathematical property of the published tariff, not an ACD statement; the 3ter/2 circular independently says 50/50 avoids a higher marginal rate.

**Grade:** statute + ACD example + recomputation. **高置信 / high** on the direction; **确定** on the two ACD-checked points.

### 2.5 2025 Class 1a reform — what it changes, and what it does not

**Fact.** From AY 2025, Art. 120bis (loi du 20 décembre 2024, Art. 4, Mémorial A 589) computes Class 1a as `Tarif(R′)` with `R′ = R − (79 380 − R)/4` for R < 79 380, plus marginal-rate caps 39/40/41/42 %. The pre-2025 method (Class 1 minus a fraction of Class 1 − Class 2) is dead. The app’s `(Class 1 + Class 2) / 2` hybrid is also dead (see [docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md](../docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md) Finding 4).

**What it does not change.** Art. 3ter al. 2 and al. 3 still assign **Class 1**, not 1a, to spouses who elect individual assessment, *even if* they have a modération or are 65+. Circulaire 3ter/1: “Un contribuable marié demandant … l’imposition individuelle dite « pure » n’est donc pas rangé en classe 1a lorsqu’il a droit à une modération d’impôt pour enfant, ni lorsqu’il a terminé sa 64e année.” Same sentence in 3ter/2 for réallocation. Circulaire 3ter/2 also notes that Art. 121 (splitting) “ne fait intervenir que la classe d’impôt 1 et ignore également le droit à la modération … ou l’âge”.

**Reform vs Class 2.** The new 1a exemption at R = €26 460 is **€0**, the same as Class 2 at €26 460 (2 × Tarif(€13 230) = 0). Above that point, Class 1a’s slope is `1.25 × marginal`, Class 2’s is `1 × marginal(R/2)`. In the first taxed band that is **10 % effective (1a) vs 8 % effective (Class 2)**. Class 2 stays at or below 1a at every tested point (table in §2.4). The 2025 reform therefore **did not** make individual/1a assessment attractive for married residents. It remains a single-parent / 65+ / widow concession.

**Evidence.** Art. 120bis LIR 2025 p. 189; loi 20.12.2024 Art. 4; circulaires 3ter/1 and 3ter/2 last paragraphs before “Compétence”; [docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md](../docs/lir-118-121-bareme-2025-2026.md).

**Grade:** statute + circulars. **确定** on “3ter ≠ 1a”; **高置信** on “Class 2 ≤ 1a after the reform” (formula + numeric checks; ACD “formules du calcul automatisé” PDF was not retrievable this run).

### 2.6 PACS / partenariat — joint request, no individual pure

**Fact.** Partners are **two distinct households, Class 1 (or 1a)** during the year. Fiches de retenue are **not** changed by the partnership; Class 2 may **not** be put on the fiche merely because they intend to ask later. Collective taxation (Class 2) **only on joint request**, if:

- common domicile/residence **the whole year**, **and**
- the partnership existed **1 January to 31 December**.

Non-residents additionally need Art. 157ter or BE convention Art. 24 §4. The request **forces assiette**. **No “individual pure”** for partners (unlike spouses). They may opt for **individual with réallocation only** (Art. 3ter al. 3 via Art. 3bis al. 5). Without any request: taxed separately on own income. Foreign PACS / Belgian cohabitation légale / German LPH until 30 Sep 2017 / any foreign registered partnership qualify if proved.

**Art. 3bis al. 2 exception:** partners in collective taxation are **not** subject to Art. 48 n° 2 (rents to close relatives collectively taxed with the exploitant) and n° 3 (rémunération of the collectively taxed spouse), nor to Art. 136–145. Married Class 2 couples **are** subject to Art. 48 n° 2–3. See §2.12.

**Evidence.** Art. 3bis, LIR 2025 p. 14; [ACD partenariat](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/p/partenariat.html); Art. 157ter al. 5 for NR partners (LIR 2026 p. 295).

**Grade:** statute + ACD. **确定 / certain.**

**Mid-year PACS ≠ Class 2 that year.** Mid-year **marriage = Class 2** that year (Art. 3 b). Mid-year **PACS ≠ Class 2** (the partnership must have existed the whole year). This is a structural difference the app must not flatten.

### 2.7 90 % rules — two different tests, do not merge

There are **two** 90 % tests. They are not the same.

#### A. Art. 3 d) — mixed resident / non-resident married couple, optional Class 2

**Test:** the **resident** spouse realises **≥ 90 % of the household’s professional income in Luxembourg** during the year. The non-resident must justify annual income. EU-official exempt salary is **not counted** in that 90 % (Note de service 119/2). If the test is met and both sign, they are collectively taxed **as if the NR spouse had been resident all year** (Art. 6 al. 4). Form 100 F 2025 p. 3 prints the declaration: “Nous déclarons qu’au moins 90 % des revenus professionnels de notre ménage … sont réalisés au Luxembourg par le contribuable résidant au Luxembourg.”

**Default if they do not (or cannot) opt:** resident spouse is **Class 1**, or **Class 1a** if the ménage has a child with modération. That 1a default can be **better than a forbidden Class 2** when the resident spouse is the low earner with children, because of CIM + 1a scale — but only if CIM’s “not both parents in the common home” test is met. If both parents live with the child, CIM is out even in 1a.

#### B. Art. 157ter — non-resident assimilation (not the Art. 3 d) test)

**Test (LIR 2026 p. 295):** NR taxpayer is taxed in Luxembourg on **≥ 90 % of worldwide income** (indigène + étranger), **or** foreign net income not subject to LU PIT is **< €13 000**. For married NR, **one** spouse meeting the test can suffice, on joint request. Belgian residents: > 50 % of household professional income in LU (convention Art. 24 §4a). Assimilation default is **Class 2** on indigène income, foreign income for taux only; Art. 3ter pure/réallocation remains available (form 100 NR boxes 409–411). 50-day commuter salary excluded from a DTT state is counted in the 90 % *up to* the non-taxable corresponding amount (al. 2).

**Evidence.** Art. 3 d), Art. 6 al. 4, Art. 157ter, LIR 2025/2026; [ACD imposition individuelle §2](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/i/imposition-individuelle.html); form 100 F 2025 pp. 3–4; NS 119/2 (EU officials).

**Grade:** statute + form. **确定.**

**App implication:** do not reuse one “90 %” flag for both. Art. 3 d) is a **professional-income-in-LU share of the ménage**. Art. 157ter is a **worldwide-income-in-LU share of the individual** (or €13 000 / BE 50 %). Different numerator, different denominator, different couples.

### 2.8 Death / divorce — 3-year Class 2 window, and it overrides 1a

**Fact.** Art. 119 n° 3 b–c: a widow(er) whose marriage ended by death, or a divorced / legally or factually separated person (dispense of law or court), stays **Class 2 for the three years preceding the tax year**, provided the divorced person had benefited from that rule (or a similar earlier one) for **five years** before the event. ACD’s recap table marks this as “période transitoire” → **Class 2, even with children, even 65+**.

**Consequence.** During the window they are **not 1a**, so **no CIM**. After the window: 1a if modération or 65+ or widow, else 1. The window is usually **barème-favourable** (Class 2 ≤ 1a, §2.4) and **CIM-unfavourable** (€3 504 refundable). That is the CIM vs Class 2 trade-off in its sharpest form.

Circulaire 3/1: people who are divorcés / séparés within the 3-year window **cannot** request Art. 3ter. They are outside Art. 3, not inside it with an option.

**Evidence.** Art. 119 n° 3, LIR 2025 p. 187; [ACD class_resid table](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/c/class_resid.html) rows “période transitoire”; circulaire 3/1 “Ainsi, plus particulièrement, les personnes divorcées … ne peuvent demander une imposition individuelle.”

**Grade:** statute + ACD + circular. **确定.**

### 2.9 Children, Class 1a vs staying Class 2, CIM

**Fact.**

- Married Class 2: children do **not** change the class (still 2). They do change plafonds (interest +672, épargne-logement, habitation household multiplier, AEP does not depend on children).
- Married Art. 3ter: children still do **not** give 1a. Modération is shared as in §2.3.
- Mixed couple without Art. 3 d): resident spouse is **1a** if a child with modération is in the ménage.
- PACS without collective request: the partner whose *ménage* has a modération child is **1a**. **Common** children: only one partner gets 1a (ACD partenariat: “Lorsqu’il s’agit d’enfants communs, le 2e partenaire n’a pas droit à la classe d’impôt 1A”).
- Divorced / veuf: 1a only **after** the 3-year Class 2 window.

**CIM (Art. 154ter, AY 2025+):** Class **1a only**, on request, max **€3 504** if adjusted taxable income < €60 000, linear to €750 at €105 000, **refundable**. Excluded: married persons even if not collectively taxed; persons who marry during the year; Class 2; partners who request collective taxation; **both parents sharing a common home with the child** (since 2017). Not a function of the number of children. Reduced by 50 % of certain child allocations above €2 712.

**CIM vs Class 2 trade-off — who actually faces it.**

| Household | Can they be 1a + CIM? | Can they be Class 2? | Trade-off? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Married residents | **No** (CIM excluded, even with 3ter) | Yes (default) | No CIM trade-off. Class 2 vs 3ter is a barème/Art. 48 question. |
| PACS, common child, common home | **No** (both parents in the home) | Yes, on request | No CIM. Class 2 usually ≥ as good (splitting + doubled forfaits). |
| PACS, child of one partner in that partner’s ménage, common home | **Yes**, if they stay separate | Yes, on request | **Yes.** Separate = 1a + CIM €3 504 refundable. Class 2 = lose CIM, gain splitting. At modest incomes CIM dominates. |
| Mixed R/NR, no Art. 3 d) | Resident spouse 1a + CIM if the NR parent is not in the common home | Only if 90 % test met | Yes, if the 90 % test is available but CIM is worth more. |
| Veuf/divorcé in 3-year window | No (forced Class 2) | Forced | After year 3: 1a + CIM appears; during the window they cannot elect into it. |

**Evidence.** Art. 154ter; [ACD CIM 2025](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/c/cim/cim2025.html); [ACD partenariat](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/p/partenariat.html); [research/2025-2026-lu-tax-credits-and-classes.md](2025-2026-lu-tax-credits-and-classes.md) §2.2 and §2.7 table.

**Grade:** statute + ACD. **确定** on eligibility; **高置信** on the trade-off ranking (CIM €3 504 refundable vs Class 2 barème saving, which is **€0** at similar incomes and material only at unequal incomes).

### 2.10 AEP €4 500 — Art. 129b, not Art. 110bis

**Fact.** The *abattement extra-professionnel* is **Art. 129b LIR**, not Art. 110bis (the gap analysis used the wrong article; fix it).

**Amount and mode:**

- Class 2 (Art. 3 or 157bis al. 3): **€4 500** / year (€375 / full month) as a *abattement de revenu imposable*, after Art. 127/127bis charges extraordinaires.
- Art. 3ter **al. 2 pure**: **€2 250 each** spouse who qualifies (€187.50 / month).
- Art. 3ter **al. 3 réallocation**: AEP stays **€4 500**, granted to the holder of the first *fiche conjoint* (ACD *abattement conjoint* page). Forfaits follow collective.

**Who qualifies (Art. 129b al. 2):**

- a) **both** spouses have professional income **Art. 14, 61, 91 or 95** (commercial, agricultural, libérale, salarié) **and** each is personally affiliated as obligatoire insured (LU or treaty foreign);
- b) one has Art. 14 or 91 income and the other is **conjoint-aidant** (CSS Art. 1er and 171);
- c) **on request**, one has a) income and the other has been receiving Art. 96 al. 1 n° 1–2 pensions for **< 3 years (36 months)** at the start of the year.

**Cap (al. 3):** granted once for all such income of the couple. Cannot exceed the **net** of the taxpayer’s own professional income, **nor** the net the other spouse would have realised if not collectively taxed. Net = Art. 14/91/95 nets (and conjoint-aidant contribution base) minus Art. 110 n° 1–2 and FDS Art. 113, and minus Art. 130 cession/cessation abatement on that income.

**Employee + side-business:** Art. 95 + Art. 14/91 satisfy al. 2 a). If the side-business is a **loss**, the net can be ≤ 0 and AEP is **capped at that net** — a loss-making side-business can wipe AEP. Conjoint-aidant status is the backdoor when the second spouse does not have own professional net.

**Evidence.** Art. 129b, LIR 2025 pp. 206–207 (full text extracted); [ACD abattement extra-professionnel](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/a/abat_extrapro.html); [ACD abattement conjoint AC](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/a/abat_conjoi.html) (withholding: AC = 540 + 480 + 4 500 = **€5 520** on the lower-earner additional fiche; FD separate); circulaire 3ter/1 AEP 2 250 section.

**Grade:** statute + ACD + circular. **确定.**

**App hook.** Do not code AEP as Art. 110bis. Do not grant AEP unless **both** have qualifying professional income (or conjoint-aidant / < 3-year pension). Respect the lower-net cap. Pure = 2 250 × 2; Class 2 / réallocation = 4 500.

### 2.11 Art. 111bis / 111ter — €3 200 (2025) / €4 500 (2026) per person, shared cap, Class 2 absorption

**Fact.**

- Art. 111bis al. 7 (LIR **2025**, p. 165): max **€3 200**/year for 111bis **or** 111ter.  
- Art. 111bis al. 7 (LIR **2026**, p. 165) and 111ter al. 7 (p. 167): max **€4 500**/year, loi du 19 décembre 2025. ACD prévoyance-vieillesse (updated 27.01.2026) confirms the 2017–2025 / 2026 split.
- **111bis and 111ter share the same cap** (“au titre d’un ou de plusieurs contrats … au sens du présent article **ou de l’article 111ter**”). You cannot stack 3 200 + 3 200 by using one of each.
- Art. 111bis al. 3 / 111ter al. 3: if collectively taxed, each spouse may have a contract; the deduction is **calculated individually** per al. 7. Household max = **cap × number of qualifying souscripteurs** (the “double 111bis”).
- Conditions: insurer/credit institution LU or EU/EEA passported; ≥ 10 years; payout age 60–75; capital, monthly life annuity, annual withdrawals, or combined. Early repayment (other than invalidity / serious illness ≥ 50 %) → Art. 99 n° 5 fully taxable (n° 4 if illness/invalidity).
- Payout: monthly life annuity **50 % exempt** Art. 115 n° 14a; capital / annual withdrawal = Art. 99 n° 4 revenus divers, demi-taux on request Art. 132 al. 2 n° 5.

**Class 2 vs individual on 111bis.** The *cap* is per person in every mode. The *absorption* is not:

- Class 2 / réallocation: both spouses’ 111bis reduce the **joint** RIA. A contract of the low-earner (or of a spouse whose only income is a small side-business) is still fully used.
- Individual **pure**: each 111bis hits that spouse’s own RIA. If that spouse’s net is too small, the unused cap is **wasted** (RIA already ≤ 0, no barème refund).

That is a Class 2 **win** when incomes are unequal and both have (or could open) 111bis contracts. It is not a reason to open a contract only to shelter the high earner: the high earner’s cap is usable in every mode.

**Evidence.** Art. 111bis/111ter LIR 2025 pp. 165–167 and LIR 2026 pp. 165–167; [ACD prévoyance-vieillesse](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/p/prevoyance_vieillesse.html): “La déduction est calculée individuellement pour les époux qui souscrivent chacun un contrat individuel.” Guichet page still showing €3 200 is **stale for 2026** (see [luxembourg-resident-tax-research.md](../luxembourg-resident-tax-research.md) §2.9).

**Grade:** statute 2025 + statute 2026 + ACD. **确定.**

**App hook.** AY 2025 returns (filed 2026) = **€3 200**. AY 2026 = **€4 500**. Shared 111bis+111ter cap per person. Do not double the cap because “classe 2”; double it because **two souscripteurs**.

### 2.12 RCP both spouses — Art. 110 n° 3 and n° 3a, and the Art. 48 trap

**Fact.** Art. 110 LIR (p. 162):

| n° | What | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Obligatory employee sickness + pension (+ treaty foreign) | Unlimited |
| 2 | Obligatory independent sickness, accidents, pension (+ treaty foreign) | Unlimited |
| 3 | Personal LRCP employee contributions (loi 8.6.1999) | **€1 200 / year** |
| 3a | Independent LRCP contributions financing retraite | **20 %** of net Art. 10 n° 1–3 (+ Art. 95 n° 6 salary under conditions, if not already used for an employer plan the person manages) |
| 4 | Continuée / voluntary / buy-back of periods | Inside FDS €480 (Art. 113) |

**Both spouses:** each has own Art. 110. Employee spouse: n° 1 (unlimited CCSS) + n° 3 (≤ €1 200 RCP). Independent spouse: n° 2 (unlimited CCSS indépendants) + n° 3a (20 % of professional net). Class 2 pools both against joint RIA — same absorption advantage as 111bis. Individual pure: each on own RIA; the independent’s n° 3a is limited by **their** net, so a loss-making side-business yields **€0** of n° 3a.

**Art. 48 n° 3 — married Class 2 business cannot deduct the spouse’s salary.** Art. 48 (p. 65): are **not** dépenses d’exploitation:

> « 2. les loyers, fermages ou redevances qui, en raison de biens affectés à l’entreprise, sont alloués à l’exploitant ou à des proches parents **imposables collectivement avec lui**;  
> 3. la rémunération allouée à l’exploitant ou **au conjoint imposable collectivement avec lui**; »

If the employee spouse is paid by the Class 2 freelance/libérale business, that salary is **not deductible**. It stays in the household as additional Art. 95 income (and may attract CIS, FFO 540, etc.), but the business cannot expense it. Net household effect is usually **negative or nil**, never the clean income-shift that a third-party salary would be.

**Art. 3ter reading.** After a valid 3ter election the spouses are **not** “imposable collectivement avec lui”. The literal condition of Art. 48 n° 3 then fails, and the salary/rent **can** be a dépense d’exploitation in the business and Art. 95/98 income in the other spouse. Circulaire 3ter/1 attributes professional income to the earner and does not restate Art. 48 n° 3 as still blocking. **No ACD worked example of this shift was found.** Treat as a **statutory reading, medium-high confidence**, worth an ACD ruling /实践经验 check before shipping as a recommendation.

**PACS exception.** Art. 3bis al. 2: articles 4–155bis apply to collective partners **except Art. 48 n° 2 and 3** (and 136–145). A PACS Class 2 couple can therefore expense partner rent/salary that a married Class 2 couple cannot. This is a **statutory** difference; again, no ACD example found.

**Evidence.** Art. 48, LIR 2025 pp. 65–66; Art. 110 p. 162; Art. 3bis al. 2 p. 14; circulaire 3ter/1 attribution rules; [lux-deductions-2025-2026-research.md](../lux-deductions-2025-2026-research.md) §1–2 on Art. 110 vs 111 vs 113.

**Grade:** statute **确定** on the text of Art. 48 n° 2–3 and the 3bis exception. Individual-assessment unlock **medium-high** (inference from the statutory condition; not observed in an ACD example).

### 2.14 Side-business specifics (employee + freelance / libérale / commerciale / e-commerce / AirBnB / YouTube)

These are the **structural** points, not invented dossiers.

1. **Profitable side-business, unequal incomes.** Class 2 cumulates Art. 95 + Art. 14/91 and splits. Convexity → Class 2 ≤ individual pure. This is the default recommendation. *Evidence:* Art. 3, Art. 121, §2.4 tariff checks.

2. **Loss-making side-business.** Art. 7 al. 2: losses of one category offset other categories of the **same** taxpayer. In Class 2 the loss reduces **household** RIA immediately (salary minus business loss). In individual pure the loss stays with the independent and may only help via Art. 114 carry-forward if there is no other own income. Class 2 wins. ACD does not publish a numeric example; the statute is enough. *Evidence:* Art. 7 al. 2, Art. 114.

3. **Spouse works in the business.** Married Class 2: Art. 48 n° 3 blocks the salary as dépense d’exploitation (§2.12). PACS Class 2: Art. 48 n° 3 does not apply. Married Art. 3ter: literal condition fails; possible unlock, unverified by ACD example.

4. **AEP.** Employee (Art. 95) + freelance (Art. 14/91) = both professional → AEP €4 500 in Class 2, but **capped by the lower net**. A €2 000 libérale net caps AEP at ~€2 000. A loss can zero it. Conjoint-aidant is the alternative.

5. **CCSS indépendants.** Art. 110 n° 2, unlimited, dépenses spéciales, not frais d’exploitation. In Class 2 they reduce joint RIA. Confirm the independent is affiliated (obligatoire) — that is also an AEP condition.

6. **CII (Art. 152ter)** vs CIS (Art. 154quater): mutually exclusive **per person**. The employee keeps CIS; the independent keeps CII; class does not merge them. Do not grant CIS on the independent’s income or CII on the salary.

7. **E-commerce / YouTube / AirBnB** classification (commercial Art. 14 vs libérale Art. 91 vs location Art. 98 vs divers Art. 99bis) changes *which* net feeds AEP al. 2 a) and n° 3a 20 %, not the Class 2 vs 3ter mechanics. Location and divers are **not** Art. 129b professional income.

8. **Filing.** Class 2 residents are not automatically in assiette unless they hit an Art. 96/97-style threshold (see [research-filing-2025-2026.md](../research-filing-2025-2026.md) §2.1: > €100 000 taxable, > €600 non-withholding, etc.). Art. 3ter, PACS collective, and Art. 3 d) **always** force modèle 100. Deadline 31 December N+1. Double signature. Assistant électronique cannot request 3ter or the mixed 90 % option.

### 2.15 Election mechanics, fiches, and what the form actually asks

**Deadline and irrevocability.** Request by **31 December N+1**. Made during N or before: still changeable until that date, unless a bulletin was issued. Several successive requests: the last conforming one wins. After the deadline: locked for that year; next year reopens. Circulaires 3ter/1 and 3ter/2 worked example: request fin décembre 2022 for 2023 → changeable until 31 décembre 2024, including on the 2023 return.

**Fiches.** Class 2 on the fiche is possible for married residents (and for the Art. 3 d) / 3bis request once made). Art. 3ter pure: Class 1 on each fiche. Art. 3ter réallocation: **no class on the fiche**; a **taux** is inscribed corresponding to Class 1 residents taxed on indigène + étranger (ACD class_resid note (*); form 100 note). Partners: no Class 2 on the fiche from intention alone.

**Form 100 F 2025 (local PDF, 20 pages):**

- p. 3 E/R: 316 header “nous demandons”; **317** collective Art. 3; **318** pure Art. 3ter(2) + cases 320–321; **319** réallocation Art. 3ter(3) + cases 322–323; mixed 90 % Art. 3 d) declaration; both signatures p. 20.
- p. 4 NR: **409** collective 157bis(3)/157ter; **410** pure; **411** réallocation; 90 % worldwide / €13 000 / BE 50 %.

**Grade:** form + circulars. **确定.**
